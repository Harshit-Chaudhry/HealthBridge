# DISEASE PROFILE
Disease Name: COVID-19
Medical Name: Coronavirus disease 2019 (caused by the virus SARS-CoV-2)
Disease Category: Infectious disease (viral); respiratory; airborne / droplet-spread
Also Known As: Coronavirus disease, COVID, novel coronavirus disease, SARS-CoV-2 infection

## 1. Overview
COVID-19 is an infectious disease caused by the virus **SARS-CoV-2**. It mainly affects the respiratory system, but it can affect other parts of the body. It was first identified in late 2019, spread worldwide, and was declared a pandemic by the World Health Organization (WHO) in March 2020. WHO ended the global public health emergency status in May 2023, but COVID-19 continues to circulate and cause illness.

Most people who get COVID-19 have mild to moderate illness and recover without special treatment. Some people become seriously ill and need hospital care, particularly older adults and people with certain underlying health conditions, though anyone can become seriously ill. Some people have symptoms that last for weeks or months, known as **post COVID-19 condition ("long COVID")**.

COVID-19 can be prevented and its severity reduced through vaccination, good ventilation, hygiene, staying home when sick, and early testing and treatment for those at higher risk. The virus keeps changing (new variants appear), so recommendations are updated over time. Please refer to WHO and national health authorities for the latest guidance.

## 2. Causes
- COVID-19 is caused by **SARS-CoV-2** (severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2), a coronavirus.
- The virus enters the body through the nose, mouth or eyes and infects cells in the respiratory tract and sometimes other organs.
- The virus mutates over time. Different **variants** have emerged, some spreading more easily or evading immunity better than earlier ones.
- Having the virus does not always cause symptoms. People can be infected and have no symptoms, yet still spread the virus.

## 3. Risk Factors
**Risk of infection:**
- Close contact with an infected person, especially in crowded, enclosed or poorly ventilated indoor spaces
- Prolonged time with someone who is infected, especially without masks or ventilation
- Not being up to date with vaccination, or low immunity from previous infection or vaccination
- Singing, shouting, or exercising in crowded indoor spaces, which release more respiratory particles
- Healthcare and caregiving settings

**Risk of severe illness:**
- Older age (risk rises with age)
- Chronic conditions such as heart disease, diabetes, chronic lung disease, chronic kidney disease, chronic liver disease, and obesity
- Weakened immune system (for example, from HIV, cancer treatment, organ transplant, or immune-suppressing medicines)
- Pregnancy
- Not being vaccinated or not up to date with vaccination
- Certain disabilities and neurological conditions
- Smoking
- Limited access to healthcare

## 4. Transmission
- **Main route:** Through the air. An infected person releases the virus in tiny droplets and smaller aerosol particles when breathing, talking, coughing, sneezing, singing or laughing. Others can breathe these in, or the particles can land on their eyes, nose or mouth.
- **Close contact and indoor spaces:** Spread is most likely at close range and in crowded, poorly ventilated indoor spaces. Aerosols can build up and remain in the air indoors.
- **Surfaces:** Touching a contaminated surface and then touching the face is considered a less common route.
- **Pre-symptomatic and asymptomatic spread:** People can spread the virus a day or two before symptoms start, and people who never develop symptoms can also spread it.
- **Incubation period:** Symptoms typically appear about 5 to 6 days after infection, within a range of about 1 to 14 days (it may be shorter with some newer variants).
- **Infectious period:** People are generally most infectious in the days around symptom onset, though this varies and people with weakened immunity can remain infectious for longer.

## 5. Symptoms

### Common Symptoms
- Fever or chills
- Cough
- Sore throat
- Runny or blocked nose
- Tiredness
- Headache
- Muscle or body aches
- Loss or change of taste or smell (less common with some newer variants)
- Shortness of breath or difficulty breathing (in more severe cases)

### Less Common Symptoms
- Diarrhoea, nausea or vomiting
- Loss of appetite
- Rash or discolouration of fingers or toes
- Red or irritated eyes (conjunctivitis)
- Aches and pains
- Dizziness
- Confusion or "brain fog"
- Chest pain or pressure
- Sleep problems
- Persistent symptoms for weeks or months (post COVID-19 condition)

Some people have no symptoms at all. COVID-19 symptoms overlap with flu, common cold and other respiratory infections, so testing is the only way to know.

### Symptoms That May Require Medical Attention
- Fever and cough that are not improving after a few days
- Difficulty breathing or shortness of breath at rest
- Symptoms in a person at higher risk of severe illness (older adult, pregnant woman, chronic condition, weak immunity)
- Symptoms that worsen after initial improvement
- Dehydration (unable to drink fluids, very little urine)
- Symptoms in infants and young children, especially if feeding poorly or unusually sleepy
- Symptoms that last for weeks and interfere with daily life

## 6. Warning Signs / Emergency Symptoms
Seek urgent medical care immediately (go to the nearest hospital or call local emergency services) if someone with COVID-19 or suspected COVID-19 has:
- Difficulty breathing or severe shortness of breath
- Persistent chest pain or pressure
- New confusion, inability to stay awake or difficulty waking
- Bluish, pale or grey lips, face or nails, or pale/mottled skin
- Loss of speech or movement, or sudden weakness
- Seizures
- Low oxygen levels (if measured with a pulse oximeter, as advised by a doctor)
- Inability to drink fluids or persistent vomiting
- Severe worsening of symptoms
- In children: fast or laboured breathing, bluish lips, extreme irritability, not waking or interacting, persistent fever with rash, or severe abdominal pain

## 7. Diagnosis
COVID-19 cannot be diagnosed reliably from symptoms alone. Testing helps confirm infection.
- **Rapid antigen tests (RATs):** Detect viral proteins, give results in minutes, and can be used at home or in clinics. They work best when symptoms are present or when viral levels are high. A negative result in someone with symptoms or exposure may need to be repeated or confirmed with another test.
- **Molecular tests (RT-PCR and other nucleic acid amplification tests, NAATs):** Detect viral genetic material from nose or throat samples. They are more sensitive than rapid antigen tests and are often used for confirmation.
- **Chest imaging and other tests:** Doctors may use chest X-ray or CT scans, blood tests, and oxygen level checks to assess how severe the illness is.
- **Antibody tests:** Show past infection or vaccination response but are not used to diagnose current infection.
- **Reporting:** Positive results may need to be reported to health authorities, depending on local rules.

## 8. Treatment and Management
*This section is general education. Treatment decisions, including whether to use antiviral medicines, are made by a doctor based on the person's age, risk factors, symptoms and how long they have been ill. Do not self-medicate.*

- **Most people recover at home.** Rest, plenty of fluids, and symptom relief (for example, medicines for fever or pain, as advised by a healthcare professional) are the mainstay for mild illness.
- **Isolation:** Stay home and away from others while sick, in line with local health guidance, and wear a well-fitting mask around others when you must be near them. Ventilate rooms well.
- **Monitoring:** Watch for warning signs (Section 6). A doctor may advise monitoring oxygen levels with a pulse oximeter for some patients.
- **Antiviral medicines:** WHO and national guidelines recommend certain antiviral medicines for people at higher risk of severe disease, typically started early in the illness (examples include nirmatrelvir/ritonavir; other antivirals are used in some settings). They are prescription medicines, and not everyone is eligible, so ask a doctor promptly if you are at higher risk.
- **Hospital care:** Severe COVID-19 may need oxygen support, steroid medicines such as dexamethasone, and other treatments given in hospital under medical supervision. Some patients need intensive care or ventilatory support.
- **Not recommended:** Antibiotics do not work against viruses and are only used if there is a separate bacterial infection diagnosed by a doctor. Medicines such as hydroxychloroquine are not recommended by WHO for treating COVID-19. Avoid unproven remedies and do not take prescription medicines without medical advice.
- **Recovery:** Most people recover within a couple of weeks, though cough and tiredness can last longer.
- **Long COVID:** People with persistent symptoms should see a doctor. Management focuses on symptom relief, gradual return to activity, and rehabilitation, tailored to the person.
- **Support:** Rest, nutrition, hydration and mental health support are important during recovery.

## 9. Prevention
- **Vaccination:** COVID-19 vaccines reduce the risk of severe illness, hospitalisation and death. WHO recommends vaccination, with priority for older adults, people with chronic conditions, people with weak immunity, pregnant women, and health workers. Keep up with national recommendations for doses and boosters, since guidance changes as variants and immunity change.
- **Ventilation:** Open windows and doors, use fans and air flow, and spend time outdoors when possible. Good ventilation reduces risk indoors.
- **Masks:** A well-fitting mask can reduce spread, particularly in crowded or poorly ventilated indoor places, in healthcare settings, when you have symptoms, or when you are at higher risk.
- **Hand hygiene:** Wash hands with soap and water, or use an alcohol-based hand rub.
- **Respiratory etiquette:** Cover coughs and sneezes with a tissue or elbow, and dispose of tissues safely.
- **Stay home when sick,** and test if you have symptoms or have been exposed.
- **Avoid crowded, closed spaces** when infection is circulating, especially if you are at higher risk.
- **Clean frequently touched surfaces** regularly (a less important route than airborne spread).
- **Early testing and treatment** for people at higher risk of severe disease.
- **Keeping up general health:** Balanced diet, physical activity, sleep, and managing chronic conditions help overall resilience.

## 10. Complications
- **Pneumonia** (infection of the lungs)
- **Acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS)** and respiratory failure
- **Blood clots** (in the legs, lungs or other organs), stroke or heart attack
- **Heart complications:** Inflammation of the heart muscle (myocarditis), abnormal heart rhythms
- **Kidney injury**
- **Sepsis and multi-organ failure** in severe cases
- **Worsening of existing conditions** such as diabetes, heart or lung disease
- **Neurological effects:** Confusion, "brain fog", headaches, and rarely stroke or other nerve problems
- **Multisystem inflammatory syndrome in children (MIS-C):** A rare but serious condition that can follow COVID-19 in children
- **Post COVID-19 condition (long COVID):** WHO describes this as symptoms that usually appear within 3 months of infection, last at least 2 months, and cannot be explained by another condition. Common symptoms include fatigue, breathlessness and cognitive difficulties ("brain fog"), and they can affect daily functioning
- **Mental health effects:** Anxiety, depression and sleep problems for some people
- **Death:** Can occur, mainly in older people and those with underlying conditions

## 11. High-Risk Groups
- Older adults
- People with chronic conditions: heart disease, diabetes, chronic lung disease, chronic kidney or liver disease, obesity, certain neurological conditions
- People with weakened immunity (for example, HIV, cancer treatment, organ transplant, immune-suppressing medicines)
- Pregnant women
- People who are unvaccinated or not up to date with vaccination
- Residents of long-term care and congregate settings
- Health workers and caregivers (exposure risk)
- People in crowded living or working conditions
- People with limited access to healthcare
- Infants and some children with underlying conditions

## 12. When to See a Doctor
- You test positive and are at higher risk of severe illness. Contact a doctor early, because antiviral treatment must usually be started soon after symptoms begin.
- You have a fever or cough that is not improving, or symptoms that are worsening
- You develop shortness of breath, chest pain, or confusion. **Go to hospital immediately** (see Section 6).
- You are pregnant, have a long-term health condition, or have weakened immunity and develop symptoms
- A baby or young child has fever, difficulty breathing, poor feeding or unusual drowsiness
- You are unable to drink fluids or are becoming dehydrated
- Symptoms continue for several weeks, or new symptoms appear after recovery (possible long COVID)
- You are unsure whether to take medicines or you need advice about vaccination, testing, or when to end isolation

## 13. Common Misconceptions
1. **"COVID-19 is just like a common cold."** Many cases are mild, but COVID-19 can cause severe illness, complications and long-term symptoms.
2. **"Only older people are affected."** Anyone can get infected and become seriously ill, though risk rises with age and certain conditions.
3. **"Antibiotics treat COVID-19."** Antibiotics do not work on viruses. They are only used if a doctor finds a separate bacterial infection.
4. **"Vaccines give you COVID-19."** COVID-19 vaccines do not contain the live virus that causes the disease and cannot give you COVID-19. Mild, short-lived side effects such as arm soreness or tiredness can occur.
5. **"If I've had COVID-19, I'm permanently immune."** Immunity from past infection or vaccination can wane and new variants can partly evade it, so reinfection is possible.
6. **"If I have no symptoms, I can't spread it."** People without symptoms can spread the virus.
7. **"A negative rapid test means I definitely don't have COVID-19."** Rapid tests can miss infection, especially early on. Repeat testing or a molecular test may be needed.
8. **"Masks and ventilation don't matter."** They reduce the spread of the virus, especially in crowded or poorly ventilated indoor spaces.
9. **"Hot drinks, gargling or herbal remedies prevent or cure COVID-19."** These have not been shown to prevent or cure COVID-19. Rely on vaccination, prevention measures and medical advice.
10. **"Long COVID only happens after severe illness."** Long COVID can follow mild illness too, though it is more common after more severe infection.

## 14. Frequently Asked Questions

**Q1. What is COVID-19?**
COVID-19 is an infectious disease caused by the virus SARS-CoV-2. It mainly affects the lungs and airways but can affect other organs too.

**Q2. How does COVID-19 spread?**
Mostly through the air, when an infected person breathes, talks, coughs or sneezes and others breathe in the virus. It spreads most easily at close range and in crowded, poorly ventilated indoor spaces.

**Q3. What are the symptoms of COVID-19?**
Fever, cough, sore throat, runny nose, tiredness, headache and body aches are common. Some people lose their sense of taste or smell. Some people have no symptoms.

**Q4. How soon after exposure do symptoms appear?**
Usually about 5 to 6 days, within a range of about 1 to 14 days.

**Q5. How is COVID-19 diagnosed?**
By testing: rapid antigen tests, or molecular tests such as RT-PCR. Symptoms alone cannot confirm COVID-19.

**Q6. How long should I stay away from others if I have COVID-19?**
Follow your doctor's and your country's current health guidance. In general, stay home while you are sick, wear a well-fitting mask around others, and ventilate rooms. Ask a healthcare professional when it is safe to return to normal activities.

**Q7. Is there a treatment for COVID-19?**
Most people recover at home with rest, fluids and symptom relief. Prescription antiviral medicines can help people at higher risk if started early, and severe cases need hospital care. Ask a doctor, and do not self-medicate.

**Q8. Do antibiotics work for COVID-19?**
No. Antibiotics do not work against viruses. They are only used if a doctor diagnoses a separate bacterial infection.

**Q9. Who is most at risk of severe COVID-19?**
Older adults, people with chronic conditions such as heart disease, diabetes, chronic lung or kidney disease, people with weak immunity, pregnant women, and people who are not vaccinated or up to date with vaccination.

**Q10. Do COVID-19 vaccines work?**
Yes. Vaccines reduce the risk of severe illness, hospitalisation and death. Protection against infection can fade over time and with new variants, so follow national guidance on boosters.

**Q11. Can I get COVID-19 more than once?**
Yes. Reinfection can happen because immunity can fade and new variants may partly evade it.

**Q12. What is long COVID?**
Long COVID (post COVID-19 condition) is when symptoms such as fatigue, breathlessness or "brain fog" continue for weeks or months after the infection. See a doctor if symptoms persist.

**Q13. Do masks and ventilation help?**
Yes. A well-fitting mask reduces the spread of the virus, and good ventilation reduces the amount of virus in the air indoors. They help most in crowded or enclosed spaces.

**Q14. Can children get COVID-19?**
Yes. Most children have mild illness, but some can become seriously ill, and rarely a serious inflammatory condition (MIS-C) can follow. Seek medical care if a child has trouble breathing, bluish lips, extreme drowsiness or persistent high fever.

**Q15. Where can I get COVID-19 testing, treatment and vaccination in India?**
At government health facilities such as primary health centres and hospitals, and at private facilities. Ask at your nearest health centre or check official Ministry of Health and Family Welfare channels for current services and recommendations.
